# Topic

## Why Teatro Stignani?

The Teatro comunale Ebe Stignani was selected as the topic of this project because it is one of the most famous cultural landmarks in Imola. Located in the historic city centre, the theater has strong artistic, civic, and symbolic value. It is not only a building, but also an important part of Imola's urban identity.

This makes it a useful case study for a project on cultural heritage knowledge graphs. A well-known monument such as the Teatro Ebe Stignani should ideally be represented in a structured and semantically rich way. However, knowledge graphs may still contain gaps, limited descriptions, or missing links between entities, subjects, creators, places, and historical context.

## Project Focus

This project focuses on how the Teatro comunale Ebe Stignani is represented in the ArCo Knowledge Graph, the official ontology network and RDF dataset for Italian cultural heritage. Through SPARQL queries, the project investigates what information is already present and what information may be missing or incomplete.

The project then uses Large Language Models, especially Claude and Grok, to test whether they can help propose new RDF triples for enriching the existing knowledge graph.

## Why This Topic Is Suitable for KG Enrichment

The Teatro comunale Ebe Stignani is suitable for this project because it allows us to investigate different types of semantic information, such as:

* the cultural institute itself;
* its label and possible alternative names;
* its subject or iconographic meaning;
* its connection to Imola;
* its creators or related agents (such as the person it is named after);
* possible missing contextual information.

The goal is not to replace the ArCo data, but to propose possible enrichment triples based on SPARQL exploration, LLM-assisted generation, and human verification.

## Objectives

The main objectives are:

1. To explore the ArCo Knowledge Graph using SPARQL.
2. To identify the most relevant ArCo resource for the Teatro comunale Ebe Stignani.
3. To detect possible information gaps in the RDF description.
4. To use Claude and Grok to propose candidate knowledge.
5. To compare the outputs of the two LLMs.
6. To create RDF triples that could enrich the existing representation.
